\begin{abstract}
The finite projective corpus contains two natural candidates for the missing fermionic
weak-isospin carrier: the canonical multiplicity-two Hecke doublets of the internal
operator algebra $\End(\Vc)$, and the exponentially rich fibre of history data erased by
the canonical projective channel.
This note proves that neither candidate, nor any sequential combination of the deposited
structures, produces that carrier at the current stratum.
Four exact obstructions are established.
First, the commutant of the finite Weil module is spanned by the two parity projectors,
so every connected unitary action commuting with the Weil action on $\Vc$ is abelian: the
operator doublets admit no fermionic lift to their own carrier.
Second, the erased history fibres are structurally abelian: the oriented Cayley frame has
stabiliser exactly $C_4$, the fibre over a $b$-shadow with $r$ zero-runs is exactly
$(\mathbb{Z}_2)^r$, and every canonical induction of the history space yields only
near-regular multiplicities with no privileged $M_2(\C)$ factor.
Third, the flip-antisymmetric fibre line does transport to the parity-odd operator sector,
but the two resulting Hermitian axes $i(\AX\pm\AY)$ are exchanged by a semilinear
automorphism of the cocycle-enriched Heisenberg--Weil datum, implemented by the deposited
antiunitary $RK$; no deposited datum selects one axis, and the coordinate formula that
does is conditional on an oriented central/Weyl pinning that is itself an additional datum.
Fourth, and centrally: the projective channel acts on amplitudes as a conditional
expectation $P$, and for every downstream linear map $Q$ one has
$Pv=0\Rightarrow QPv=0$, so no tower of sequential projections or subsequent linear
processing can recover the alternating fibre character erased at the first stratum.
The corollary is an architectural dichotomy: any realisation of the history-derived odd
operator requires a parallel channel from the pre-projection space together with an
independent orientation of the central and Weyl generators --- two new axioms, hence an
extension architecture rather than a derivation of the deposited corpus.

\noindent\textbf{Interpretive status.}
All results are finite, exact, and machine-certified by three archived scripts.
The note closes derivation routes; it does not exclude an explicitly axiomatised parallel
channel, and it leaves the fermionic multiplicity target itself untouched and open.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.}
Finite Weil representation; Heisenberg group; multiplicity doublets; trajectory

\section{Problem, Results, and Epistemic Scope}
\label{sec:introduction}

The fermionic front of the programme is gated by one representation-theoretic target.
The Standard-Model matter sector requires a bundle of the shape
\[
  H_{\mathrm{matter}}=(S_L\otimes M_L)\oplus(S_R\otimes M_R),
\]
with $S_{L/R}$ the Lorentz Weyl modules, $M_L\cong\C^2$ carrying a non-trivial internal
action commuting with the Lorentz action, and $M_R$ a sum of trivial lines.
On an irreducible Weyl module the Lorentz commutant is scalar, so the multiplicity factor
$M_L$ cannot live on the spinor factor itself~\cite{Beau2026q14}; it must live on an
indexing space of copies, downstream of the projection~\cite{Beau2026m}.

Two deposited structures look like candidates for that indexing space.
The first is the internal operator algebra of the finite Weil module: O33 proves that
$\End(\Vc)$ decomposes as $\Id\oplus\Dplus\oplus\Dminus$ under conjugation and that its
generic principal-series constituents occur in canonical multiplicity-two pairs with full
Hecke commutant $M_2(\C)$~\cite{Beau2026a37}.
The second is the space of projective histories: the trajectory-branching note proves
that the canonical O12-compatible channel retains only the $b$-shadow of a
non-backtracking Heisenberg word, so that the erased fibre --- the central register and
the sign data of the $X$-steps --- grows exponentially while endpoint data grow
polynomially~\cite{Beau2026tb}.
Erased histories over one retained record are literally indexed copies, which is the
shape the multiplicity target requires.

This note determines exactly what these two structures can and cannot supply.
The results are negative in a structured way.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item The operator doublets have no fermionic lift: the commutant of $\Vc$ is spanned
  by the parity projectors, so no non-abelian connected action commutes with the Weil
  action on the carrier itself (Section~\ref{sec:no-lift}).
  \item The erased fibres are abelian and their canonical inductions are structureless:
  frame stabiliser $C_4$, fibre gauge $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^r$, near-regular induced
  multiplicities (Section~\ref{sec:fibres}).
  \item The alternating fibre line transports to the protected odd operator sector, but
  the two candidate odd Hermitian axes are exchanged by a semilinear automorphism of the
  cocycle-enriched datum; no natural selector exists (Section~\ref{sec:axes}).
  \item No sequential re-entry exists: the channel acts as a conditional expectation $P$,
  and $Pv=0\Rightarrow QPv=0$ for every downstream linear $Q$, so the erased alternating
  character is unrecoverable by any projection tower
  (Section~\ref{sec:no-reentry}).
\end{enumerate}

The paper separates three epistemic levels.
Proved statements are finite and exact, with self-contained proofs.
Machine-certified statements are exact computations executed by the three archived
scripts of Section~\ref{sec:certification}.
Interpretive statements --- in particular any weak-sector reading of the operator
doublets --- are labelled as such and never promoted.
The note closes derivation routes inside the deposited corpus; it does not prove that no
extension of the corpus can realise the carrier, and it formulates the exact axioms such
an extension would have to add (Corollary~\ref{cor:dichotomy}).

This note is the closure of a longer audited chain.
Upstream of it, the corpus already contains the Lorentz lock (on an irreducible Weyl
module no internal non-abelian factor commutes with the Lorentz
action~\cite{Beau2026q14}), the equivariance kill-switch (no fully metaplectic
multiplicity-bearing eliminated block exists inside $\Vc$~\cite{Korinman2013}), and the
complete stabiliser of the deposited filtration (abelian, with no induced non-abelian
selector at the finite stratum~\cite{Beau2026a37}).

\section{Finite Setting and Deposited Structures}
\label{sec:setting}

Let $q$ be an odd prime and $\psi_c(t)=\exp(2\pi ict/q)$ for $c\in\Fq^*$.
Write elements of $\Heis$ as $(a,b,z)$ with product
\begin{equation}
  (a,b,z)(a',b',z')=(a+a',b+b',z+z'+ab').
  \label{eq:heis-product}
\end{equation}
The Schr\"odinger representation on $\Vc=\C^{\Fq}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\rho_c(a,b,z)f\bigr)(x)=\psi_c\bigl(z+b(x-a)\bigr)f(x-a),
  \label{eq:schrodinger}
\end{equation}
with symmetrically ordered displacements
$W_c(a,b)f(x)=\psi_c\bigl(b(x-a/2)\bigr)f(x-a)$ and the exact relation
\begin{equation}
  \rho_c(a,b,z)=\psi_c\bigl(z-ab/2\bigr)\,W_c(a,b).
  \label{eq:rho-weyl}
\end{equation}
The parity operator is $(Rf)(x)=f(-x)$, the plain conjugation is $K$, and the deposited
Weil model pins the Fourier operator $F[x,y]=q^{-1/2}\psi_c(xy)$~\cite{Korinman2013,
Beau2026a37}.
Under conjugation, $\End(\Vc)\cong\Id\oplus\Dplus\oplus\Dminus$ with $\Dplus$ the
$R$-even and $\Dminus$ the $R$-odd displacement span, and the generic multiplicity-two
Hecke structure of $\Dplus$ and $\Dminus$ is as established in O33~\cite{Beau2026a37}.

Trajectories are non-backtracking words $\gamma=(g_1,\dots,g_n)$ in the alphabet
$S=\{X,X^{-1},Y,Y^{-1}\}$, $X=(1,0,0)$, $Y=(0,1,0)$, identified with paths from the
identity~\cite{Beau2026tb}.
The $b$-emission of a letter is $0$ for $X^{\pm1}$ and $\pm1$ for $Y^{\pm1}$; the
$b$-shadow of a word is its emission sequence.
The canonical channel is $b$-only: every mono-path norm profile factors through the
$b$-shadow, the central register is unconditionally erased, and the erased fibre of the
canonical projective distinguishability is exactly the central register together with the
$X$-sign data~\cite{Beau2026tb}.
The number of $b$-shadows of length $n$ is the Pell count
$N_b(n)=2N_b(n-1)+N_b(n-2)$, $N_b(1)=3$, $N_b(2)=7$~\cite{Beau2026tb}.
At the amplitude level, the projective action of the channel is the conditional
expectation onto the record $\sigma$-algebra~\cite{Beau2026pce}.

\section{No Fermionic Lift of the Operator Doublets}
\label{sec:no-lift}

The Hecke doublets of $\End(\Vc)$ live at the operator stratum.
The first obstruction states that they cannot be pulled back to a commuting action on the
carrier $\Vc$ itself.

\begin{theorem}[Abelian carrier commutant]
  \label{thm:abelian-commutant}
  Let $\rho$ denote the finite Weil representation of $\G$ on $\Vc$ extending the
  Heisenberg action~\eqref{eq:schrodinger}.
  Then
  \begin{equation}
    \End_{\rho}(\Vc)=\C P_+\oplus\C P_-,
    \label{eq:weil-commutant}
  \end{equation}
  where $P_\pm$ are the parity projectors.
  Consequently every connected unitary group action on $\Vc$ commuting with $\rho$ lies
  in the diagonal torus $\{\lambda_+P_++\lambda_-P_-:|\lambda_\pm|=1\}$ and is abelian.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The even and odd parity submodules of the odd-prime Weil representation are
  irreducible and mutually inequivalent~\cite{Korinman2013}, and the deposited model
  generators match Korinman's up to the exact convention translation recorded
  in~\cite{Beau2026a37}.
  Schur's lemma gives~\eqref{eq:weil-commutant}.
  A connected unitary subgroup of the invertibles of a two-dimensional commutative
  $C^*$-algebra is a subgroup of its unitary group $U(1)\times U(1)$, hence abelian.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No lift]
  \label{cor:no-lift}
  No $SU(2)$ action on $\Vc$ commutes with the Weil action.
  In particular the canonical $M_2(\C)$ multiplicity algebras of $\Dplus$ and
  $\Dminus$ inside $\End(\Vc)$~\cite{Beau2026a37} are not the image of a commuting
  carrier action: any fermionic realisation requires a new tensor factor or a new
  carrier, not present in $\Vc$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Antiunitary refinement]
  \label{rem:antiunitary}
  For $q\equiv1\pmod4$, right translation by a square root $i$ of $-1$ composed with the
  natural exchange real structure gives an antiunitary $J$ with $J^2=\theta(-1)\Id$ on
  the generic $\theta$-doublets: $J^2=+\Id$ on the dynamically split even sector and
  $J^2=-\Id$ only on the protected odd sector.
  The quaternionic sign therefore sits exactly on the sector that the deposited even
  algebra cannot activate, and it does not transport to a matter carrier.
\end{remark}

\section{Erased History Fibres Are Structurally Abelian}
\label{sec:fibres}

The second candidate is the erased fibre itself.
Its exact structure is as follows.

\begin{lemma}[Frame stabiliser]
  \label{lem:frame-stabiliser}
  The stabiliser in $\G$ of the phase-space alphabet $\{\pm e_1,\pm e_2\}$ is
  $\langle w\rangle\cong C_4$ with $w=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  A stabilising matrix permutes four vectors spanning the plane, so each basis vector
  maps to $\pm e_1$ or $\pm e_2$ and the matrix is monomial with entries $\pm1$.
  The diagonal ones with determinant one are $\pm I$; the antidiagonal ones are $w$ and
  $w^3$.
\end{proof}

A single-alphabet history space is therefore not a canonical $\G$-carrier: any canonical
object must be completed over the frame orbit, and the completion is controlled by
induction from $C_4$.

\begin{proposition}[Fibre structure]
  \label{prop:fibre-structure}
  A non-backtracking word of length $n$ is equivalent to the pair consisting of its
  $b$-shadow and one sign per maximal zero-run of that shadow.
  The fibre over a shadow with $r$ zero-runs has exactly $2^r$ elements, and
  \begin{equation}
    \sum_{\sigma}2^{r(\sigma)}=4\cdot3^{n-1},
    \label{eq:fibre-sum}
  \end{equation}
  the sum running over the $N_b(n)$ shadows.
  The displacement and the central register are functions of the pair.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  Non-backtracking forbids the immediate reversal $X^{\pm1}X^{\mp1}$, so all letters of
  a maximal zero-run carry one common sign, and that sign is unconstrained by the
  adjacent letters because both $X$ and $X^{-1}$ may follow $Y^{\pm1}$ and precede
  $Y^{\pm1}$.
  Conversely the shadow determines the word once each run sign is chosen.
  Equation~\eqref{eq:fibre-sum} is then the word count, and the endpoint is a function
  of the word.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Induction abundance]
  \label{prop:abundance}
  The $C_4$ frame rotation acts freely on non-backtracking words, so
  \begin{equation}
    \Ind_{C_4}^{\G}\C[\mathrm{Words}_n]\cong3^{\,n-1}\,\C[\G],
    \label{eq:regular}
  \end{equation}
  and every irreducible $\pi$ occurs with multiplicity $3^{n-1}\dim\pi$.
  The central element $-I$ acts on shadows by global negation with exactly one fixed
  point, so
  \begin{equation}
    m_\pi\bigl(\Ind_{C_2}^{\G}\C[\mathrm{Shadows}_n]\bigr)
    =\tfrac{1}{2}\,(\dim\pi)\bigl(N_b(n)+\varepsilon_\pi\bigr),
    \label{eq:shadow-mult}
  \end{equation}
  with $\varepsilon_\pi$ the central sign.
  Neither induction possesses a privileged $M_2(\C)$ factor.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The rotation $X\to Y\to X^{-1}\to Y^{-1}$ fixes no letter, and its square inverts
  every letter, so no word is fixed by a non-trivial power: the permutation action is
  free and $\C[\mathrm{Words}_n]$ is a multiple of the regular $C_4$-module, giving
  \eqref{eq:regular}.
  A negation-fixed shadow has every emission zero, and the all-zero shadow is unique;
  Frobenius reciprocity gives~\eqref{eq:shadow-mult}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Abelian fibre gauge]
  \label{prop:fibre-gauge}
  Flipping the sign of one maximal zero-run is a channel-preserving involution of the
  history space.
  The canonical symmetry group of the fibre over a shadow with $r$ runs is exactly the
  abelian group $(\mathbb{Z}_2)^r$ generated by these flips; the frame rotation moves
  the shadow and adds no fibre-preserving element.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  A per-run flip preserves the emission sequence and non-backtracking by
  Proposition~\ref{prop:fibre-structure}, and distinct flips commute because they act on
  disjoint letters.
  Any channel symmetry fixing the shadow permutes its fibre $\{\pm1\}^r$ compatibly with
  the run structure, hence lies in the flip group.
\end{proof}

Together with Corollary~\ref{cor:no-lift}, Propositions~\ref{prop:abundance}
and~\ref{prop:fibre-gauge} close the direct multiplicity route: the fibre supplies
indexed copies, but no non-abelian action on them, and its canonical completions are
representation-theoretically structureless.

\section{The Alternating Line and the Two-Axis Ambiguity}
\label{sec:axes}

The fibre nevertheless carries one distinguished linear object.
On the length-one fibre $\{X,X^{-1}\}$, the flip-antisymmetric combination transports
through the endpoint operators to
\begin{equation}
  \rho_c(X)-\rho_c(X^{-1})=W_c(1,0)-W_c(-1,0)=:\AX,
  \label{eq:alternating-transport}
\end{equation}
an exact identity by~\eqref{eq:rho-weyl} since $b=0$ and $z=0$; and
$R\,\AX\,R=-\AX$, so $\AX$ lies in the protected odd sector $\Dminus$.
The frame orbit adds $\AY=W_c(0,1)-W_c(0,-1)$, and the odd Harper candidates are the two
Hermitian axes
\begin{equation}
  T_\pm=i\,(\AX\pm\AY).
  \label{eq:two-axes}
\end{equation}
Whether the corpus can select one axis is the orientation half of the re-entry problem.
It cannot.

\begin{theorem}[Semilinear exchanger]
  \label{thm:exchanger}
  The map $\sigma_X(a,b,z)=(-a,b,-z)$ is an automorphism of $\Heis$, and the deposited
  antiunitary $RK$ implements it:
  \begin{equation}
    (RK)\,\rho_c(a,b,z)\,(RK)^{-1}=\rho_c(-a,b,-z).
    \label{eq:rk-intertwining}
  \end{equation}
  Moreover $(RK)F(RK)^{-1}=F^{-1}$, and on the odd plane
  $RK$ maps $T_+$ to $T_-$.
  Hence the two axes are exchanged by a semilinear automorphism of the cocycle-enriched
  Heisenberg--Weil datum that preserves the oriented $b$-shadow.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The automorphism property is the identity $-(z+z'+ab')=(-z)+(-z')+(-a)b'$.
  In the position basis, $K$ conjugates the matrix entries of~\eqref{eq:schrodinger} and
  $R$ reverses the argument, giving~\eqref{eq:rk-intertwining} by direct computation;
  the same computation on the Fourier kernel $q^{-1/2}\psi_c(xy)$ gives
  $(RK)F(RK)^{-1}=\bar F^{\,R}=F^\dagger=F^{-1}$.
  On the plane, $K$ fixes the real matrix $\AX$ and conjugates $\AY$ to $-\AY$, $R$
  negates both, and antilinearity reverses the factor $i$; composing gives
  $T_+\mapsto T_-$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[No natural selector]
  \label{cor:no-selector}
  In the strict channel (norm profiles only) and in the abstract cocycle-enriched
  setting, no deposited datum distinguishes $T_+$ from $T_-$: the exchanger of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:exchanger} preserves all such data.
  The coordinate formula $T_+=i\,(\AX+F\AX F^{-1})$ does select an axis, robustly under
  every residual fibre relabelling and with odd dependence on $c$, but only conditionally
  on fixing the named central coordinate and the named Weyl generator pointwise --- an
  additional orientation datum, not a consequence of retaining the cocycle.
  The canonical channel observable moreover pairs $c$ with $q-c$, and the central flip
  exchanges the paired factors, reinforcing the obstruction at the paired level.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Support obstruction]
  \label{rem:support}
  The relative sign cannot come from richer single fibres either: a fibre transport is
  supported on the single line $b=b(\sigma)$ of phase space, so no fibre carries
  components on both $\AX$ ($b=0$) and $\AY$ ($b=\pm1$) simultaneously.
  Independent fibres contribute independent sign ambiguities, and only unoriented lines
  survive.
\end{remark}

\section{The No-Sequential-Re-entry Theorem}
\label{sec:no-reentry}

The remaining hope is a legitimate re-entry: a deposited linear map from the history
space to $\End(\Vc)$ that restores the erased fibre data downstream of the channel.
The central theorem excludes every sequential construction.

\begin{theorem}[No sequential re-entry]
  \label{thm:no-reentry}
  Let $P$ be any linear map on the history space that is constant on the fibres of the
  retained record --- in particular the amplitude-level conditional expectation of the
  canonical channel~\cite{Beau2026pce}.
  Then $P$ annihilates every flip-alternating fibre vector, while the endpoint transport
  \eqref{eq:alternating-transport} is non-zero on it.
  Consequently, for every downstream linear map $Q$,
  \begin{equation}
    Pv=0\ \Longrightarrow\ QPv=0,
    \label{eq:no-reentry}
  \end{equation}
  so no composition $Q_k\cdots Q_1P$ of subsequent linear maps, second projections, or
  conditional expectations recovers the alternating character, and no endpoint-odd map
  factors through the retained record.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  A fibre-constant map sends the two members of the length-one fibre $\{X,X^{-1}\}$ to
  the same value, so their difference maps to zero; the same holds for every
  flip-alternating vector because alternation is odd under some flip while $P$ is even
  under all of them.
  The transport~\eqref{eq:alternating-transport} equals $\AX\neq0$ on that difference,
  so it does not factor through $P$.
  Implication~\eqref{eq:no-reentry} is linearity, and it propagates along any tower by
  induction.
\end{proof}

The theorem is elementary; its force is architectural.
It applies to every lever the corpus offers, and the three deposited candidates fail it
in complementary ways.

\begin{proposition}[O33 supplies a codomain, not an arrow]
  \label{prop:o33-lever}
  The internal-carrier isomorphism $\delta_v\mapsto W_c(v)$ of~\cite{Beau2026a37}
  identifies $\C[\Fq^2]$ with $\End(\Vc)$ as conjugation modules and exhibits
  $\Dminus$ inside a deposited operator algebra.
  Its source is the phase-space label, not a history, a shadow, or an erased fibre; and
  the channel identifies $[X]$ with $[X^{-1}]$ while the endpoint transport separates
  them, so the transport cannot factor through the channel record.
  O33 therefore supplies the codomain of a would-be re-entry and neither the re-entry
  morphism nor an orientation of the pair $(Z,w)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[EBJ consumes, it does not lift]
  \label{prop:ebj-lever}
  The eliminated-block jet~\cite{Beau2026ebj} starts from a Lorentzian projector family
  $1-P(s)=Q_0+sQ_1+\cdots$ with a given antiunitary chiral grading $J_\Pi$, and its odd
  generator is the transported equivariance-defect rate.
  No deposited map sends a history, a shadow, a central register, or an element of
  $\Dminus$ into $P(s)$ or its tangent; and the chiral grading $J_\Pi$ and the
  phase-space antiunitary $RK$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:exchanger} are differently typed,
  with no transport theorem identifying their orientations.
  EBJ can consume an already constructed odd deformation; it neither re-enters the fibre
  nor orients the axes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Architectural dichotomy]
  \label{cor:dichotomy}
  Within the deposited corpus, the history-derived odd operator is unrealisable: the
  operator carrier exists (Proposition~\ref{prop:o33-lever}), the erased data exist
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:fibre-structure}), and every deposited bridge between them is
  closed (Theorems~\ref{thm:exchanger} and~\ref{thm:no-reentry},
  Proposition~\ref{prop:ebj-lever}).
  Any realisation must add, as explicit new axioms:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
    \item a parallel linear channel from the pre-projection history or substrate space,
    refining the observable record rather than post-processing it; and
    \item an orientation of the central and Weyl generators distinguishing $(Z,w)$ from
    $(-Z,w^{-1})$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Such a construction would be an extension architecture, not a derivation.
\end{corollary}

\section{Computational Certification}
\label{sec:certification}

Three scripts are archived with this note in \texttt{scripts/} and rerun in about one
second in total on the standard library and NumPy.

\texttt{front\_hist\_fibre\_doubling.py} certifies Sections~\ref{sec:fibres}
and~\ref{sec:axes} upstream: the frame stabiliser by exhaustive scan over all of $\G$
at $q=13,29,53$; the fibre bijection, the $2^r$ law, and the Pell shadow counts through
$n=10$; the freeness of the $C_4$ action and the negation fixed-point count through
$n=8$; the per-run flip involutions through $n=8$; and the exact transport
